**Causal Map for Zotero: turn a paper into a causal map**

If you keep your reading in Zotero, you can now turn any paper there into a causal map in a couple of clicks, for free.

**First, what is Zotero?**

Zotero is a free, open reference manager. Researchers use it to collect papers, keep the PDFs, and cite them as they write. If you have ever wrestled a pile of PDFs and a bibliography into order, Zotero is the tool that does it. It is run by a non-profit, it costs nothing, and a good part of the evaluation and research world already uses it every day.

**What the plugin does**

The Causal Map plugin adds one item to Zotero's right-click menu. Pick a paper that has a PDF, choose **Make a Causal Map**, and Causal Map reads the paper and draws a map of what it says causes what. Each box is a factor, each arrow is a causal claim, and every arrow keeps the quote from the paper that backs it.

So instead of reading a paper to reconstruct its argument, you see the whole web of causes and effects at once: which factors drive the main outcomes, which come up most often, and which sentence supports each link.

**How to get it**

1. Download the plugin from the [Causal Map for Zotero release page](https://github.com/stevepowell99/causalmap-zotero/releases/latest/download/causalmap-zotero.xpi).
2. In Zotero, open **Tools, Plugins**, click the gear, choose **Install Plugin From File**, and pick the file.
3. Restart Zotero.

Zotero plugins need no signing, so it installs straight away and updates itself when a new version comes out.

**It is free**

A new account gets free AI credits every month, enough for about 300 pages of coding on the default model. Signing up takes one click and there is nothing to pay. Heavier use needs a paid plan.

Pick a PDF, click twice, walk around the block, and come back to a causal map.

Try it at [app.causalmap.app](https://app.causalmap.app).
